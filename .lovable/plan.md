# Approach to Landlords Association Northern Ireland (LANI)

## Strategy

Position the approach as **research and feedback**, not a request for endorsement. LANI is a landlord body; asking them to bless a product early is the wrong opening. The right opening is: "We are building a tool for your members — what would actually help, and what would worry you?"

This also protects Roost legally. LANI will be sensitive to anything that looks like it displaces landlord responsibility, vets tenants, or gives legal advice. The positioning must repeat the Roost boundary: **Roost assists, it never decides.**

## Key messages

1. **Who it is for.** Small self-managing landlords in NI with one or two properties, currently running tenancies on messages and spreadsheets.
2. **What it does.** A single workspace for listings, applications, leases, deposits, rent, inventory, maintenance, and records — with NI law built in (LRN, Fair Fuel, Tenancy Information Notice, notice-to-quit periods, 28-day deposit rules).
3. **What it does not do.** It does not vet tenants, run credit/employment checks, give legal advice, or replace the landlord's own decisions. The landlord chooses who gets the property.
4. **What is missing.** Safety certificate vault and end-of-tenancy workflow are in progress; tax summary is planned. Identity verification is built but will not go live until a real provider is wired in.
5. **What we are asking for.** Honest feedback from the LANI team and, if possible, a small group of members willing to test the beta.

## Suggested subject line

> Roost.ni — admin tool for self-managing NI landlords: seeking feedback, not endorsement

## Draft email

```text
Dear [Name],

I am writing to introduce Roost.ni, a new tenancy-management tool being built specifically for the small self-managing landlord in Northern Ireland.

We are not a letting agent, a tenant-scoring service, or a legal-advice provider. Roost is a workspace that helps landlords advertise a property, receive applications, issue a NI-compliant tenancy agreement, record the deposit, track rent, manage maintenance, and keep the paperwork in one place — with NI law built in from the start (LRN, Tenancy Information Notice, Fair Fuel, 28-day deposit rule, Notice to Quit periods, etc.).

The landlord still makes every decision: who to accept, what checks to carry out, and how to manage the property. Roost does not vet tenants, run credit or employment checks, or recommend one applicant over another. We are clear that the letting decision remains the landlord's own.

Roost is currently in closed beta. Before we open it more widely, we would value feedback from landlord representatives who understand the day-to-day pressures of self-managing a tenancy in Northern Ireland.

We would be grateful for a short conversation or written feedback on three questions:

1. For a self-managing NI landlord, which admin tasks cause the most friction or compliance risk?
2. What would make you comfortable recommending a landlord try a digital tool — and what would make you uncomfortable?
3. Are there NI-specific requirements (e.g. safety certificates, notice periods, deposit handling) that most generic landlord tools get wrong?

If useful, we can also offer a small group of LANI members early access to the beta with no obligation.

I have attached a one-page summary of where the product is today and what is still in progress.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Your role]
Roost.ni
[Contact details]
```

## One-page summary to attach

A printable one-page document (see the existing `.lovable/plan/roost-status-report-for-a-lani-approach-2026-08-10.md`) covering:

- What Roost is and is not
- Built today

- Gaps before a wider launch
- Suggested ask of LANI

## Follow-up cadence

1. **First email:** introduction + one-page summary (no request for meeting yet).
2. **If no reply in 7 days:** short follow-up with one specific question from the list above.
3. **If reply:** offer a 20-minute call or written feedback, whichever they prefer.
4. **After feedback:** send a thank-you note and a short update when the gaps they flagged are closed.

## What to avoid

- Asking for a formal endorsement or "partnership" in the first contact.
- Using the word "Verified" for anything except the paid identity check.
- Implying Roost replaces a solicitor, agent, or the landlord's statutory duties.
- Claiming any user numbers, demand stats, or social proof that cannot be evidenced.
- Promising features that are not yet built (live identity verification, safety certificate vault, etc.).

## Decision points

- Should we send the email from a founder address or a generic `hello@roostni.co.uk`?
- Do we want to offer a LANI-specific invite code or simply direct them to `/beta`?
- Should we attach the one-page summary as PDF or a short link to `/beta`?
